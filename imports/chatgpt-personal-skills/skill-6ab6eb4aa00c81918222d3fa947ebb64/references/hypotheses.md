# Hypotheses

## H0 — ordinary display

Default. Crowded windows of glass, saints, animals, and souvenir hats are a known vernacular form (front-window collection, home altar visible to the street, thrift assemblage). Repeated color pairs are typical of people who buy sets. A printed testimony card is testimony, not ciphertext.

Accept H0 when

- objects are commercially common figurines and vessels
- readable text is plain language
- arrangement follows shelves and muntins rather than a non-physical grid
- no second plaintext reconstructs from order, color, or initials

## H1 — second message

Allowed only after a reconstruction.

Examples that would count

- first letters of independently named objects, in locked reading order, spell a grammatical sentence that is not already printed on a card
- a house number plus a dated plaque plus a mapped cipher table supplied by the user produce a second string
- identical tokens sit on a chess-like grid whose coordinates match a book-cipher index the user provided

Examples that do not count

- "many religious figures, therefore a prophecy"
- "purple appears twice, therefore royalty"
- "a cowboy hat next to an angel, therefore a named person"
- any reading that requires objects the pixels do not show

## Report shape

1. One paragraph H0
2. One paragraph H1 test, including the reconstruction attempt and why it fails or passes
3. Alternative ordinary readings (shrine, collection, I-Spy for walkers, commemorative corner)
4. Explicit non-claim — no occupant diagnosis, no intelligence narrative from figurines
