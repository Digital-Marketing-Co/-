# Reproducible research protocol

## Preregistration
Record date/time/timezone, cutoff, question set, language coverage, sources, reviewers, available platforms and budget. Questions: what information do signals convey; what evidence distinguishes associations from communication; how context changes inference; what humane training follows; which populations are missing? Defaults: English full texts, no start-date restriction, search through the current date. Log translation coverage explicitly. Never assume English captures the global literature.

Eligible: domestic canine communication or canine learning/welfare studies; comparative canid work only when transfer limits are explicit; peer-reviewed original research, systematic/narrative reviews, scholarly books, veterinary/professional guidance and government/university publications relevant to the questions. Exclude anecdotal commercial claims from empirical evidence, duplicates, inaccessible results that cannot support their proposed claim, and noncanine studies presented as canine demonstrations. Keep inaccessible potentially eligible items in a pending register. Retain preprints only as provisional and deduplicate later publications.

## Search architecture
Where actually accessible, query PubMed, Crossref, OpenAlex, university repositories, publisher sites and disciplinary databases. List subscription databases as unavailable if not searched; web search is not a substitute for claiming a database search.
Use query families `(dog OR canine OR Canis familiaris) AND (communication OR signaling OR posture OR facial OR tail OR bark OR growl OR whine OR howl OR olfactory OR scent OR urine OR play OR gesture OR pointing OR social referencing OR multimodal)` and `(dog OR canine) AND (learning OR training OR reinforcement OR shaping OR desensitization OR counterconditioning OR welfare OR separation OR pain OR socialization OR aging OR sensory impairment)`.
Search Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Columbia, Cornell, Brown, Dartmouth and Pennsylvania, together with relevant global institutions (e.g., ELTE, Vienna, Bari, Lincoln, Portsmouth, Helsinki/Jyväskylä, Kyoto, São Paulo). Absence of indexed results is not absence of evidence. Prestige has zero weight in quality grading.

## Passes and audit trail
Pass A: map domains and obtain reviews/guidelines. Pass B: select original foundational/recent studies; inspect reference lists backward and forward citing records where accessible. Pass C: target contradictory results, free-ranging/shelter dogs, disability, seniors, morphology, other-animal interactions and underrepresented regions. Pass D+: address remaining high-impact uncertainties. Record exact queries, date, engine/database, limits, inspected records, decision reasons, newly eligible records, claim changes and unfulfilled tasks. Do not confuse number of results returned with number screened.
Predefine saturation as two consecutive targeted passes with zero materially new eligible findings across every domain and no unresolved high-impact gap capable of changing recommendations. Material means new outcome, population, design, contradiction or correction. Otherwise stop only for a recorded resource/access limit and state saturation not reached. Never label this formal systematic review without reproducible database searches, deduplication, documented full eligibility accounting, appropriate independent assessment, risk-of-bias procedure and reporting requirements.

## Updating
Keep source IDs stable; deduplicate by normalized DOI or title/authors/year. Record access date and retrieval hash. Check corrections/retractions on original publisher records. Search new date interval and unresolved domains. Rebuild only claims affected by changed evidence and their dependent training plans; regenerate citations and PDF when content changes. Rendering alone must preserve search cutoff.
