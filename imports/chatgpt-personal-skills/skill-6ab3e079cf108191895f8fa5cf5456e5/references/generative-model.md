# Generative model (monograph Ch. 9)

Source — *The Science of Humor and the Architecture of the Funny*, Chapter 9.

Comic construction is a sequence of constrained transformations, not inspiration alone.

## Six operators

1. Base model — what the audience expects the subject, scene, or sentence to do.
2. Violation operator — ambiguity, reversal, exaggeration, category error, taboo, status drop, causal impossibility, or norm breach. Linguistic cousins include script opposition, logical mechanism, garden-path inference, and false analogy.
3. Benignness operator — fiction, play signal, psychological distance, consent, self-targeting, implausibility, low stakes, or an alternative norm that keeps the violation playable.
4. Delivery architecture — setup-punch, escalation, callback, visual reveal, pause, cut, repetition, or character reaction.
5. Social alignment — who is laughing with whom and at whom.
6. Aftereffects — affiliation, licensed criticism, embarrassment, or harm. Prefer affiliation and entertainment.

## Minimal comic unit

prediction → violation → appraisal → interpretation → affect → social display

The arrow is not a rigid serial circuit. Timing, deadpan, and a later callback can change appraisal before the reader finishes the first parse. Absurdist humor may withhold resolution and still work if the mismatch stays play-framed.

## Timing

A punch before the predictive model stabilizes has nothing to violate. A punch after the reader has already guessed it is dead. Leave a beat — a short sentence, a paragraph break, a withheld clause — so inference can finish.

## GTVH knowledge resources (verbal beats)

When a line is a joke inside the story, check six resources

- script opposition
- logical mechanism
- situation
- target
- narrative strategy
- language

Do not name these in the story. Use them to choose the line.
