# High-yield mechanisms (monograph Ch. 8)

Load at least five named forms per story. Prefer this short list. The full 155-entry catalogue is in `science-of-humor.pdf` if a rarer form fits better.

## Engines (pick one primary)

- Incongruity — expectation vs scene
- Incongruity-resolution — setup script, punch script, recoverable bridge
- Benign-violation — norm breach that stays playable
- Script-opposition — two overlapping opposed scripts in one text
- Bisociation / conceptual blending — two frames in one image or line
- Play-frame — metacommunication that the attack is not literal

## Linguistic and rhetorical

- Pun, homophonic pun, homographic pun, polysemous pun
- Syntactic or pragmatic ambiguity
- Literalization of idiom
- Malapropism, spoonerism, mondegreen, eggcorn (character voice, not narrator default)
- Zeugma
- Paraprosdokian
- Garden-path
- Anti-joke and meta-humor (only when the subject is already joke-shaped)
- Double entendre and innuendo (adult subjects only; never involving minors)
- Understatement, overstatement, hyperbole, litotes
- Bathos, anticlimax, comic reversal, role-reversal
- Irony, verbal irony, sarcasm-lite, deadpan

## Structure and performance

- Callback, running gag
- Rule of three
- Misdirection
- Escalation, heightening
- Tag joke, one-liner, riff
- Timing, pause-based
- Breaking character, fourth-wall (use once, not as the whole story)

## Genre color (match the subject)

- Observational, character comedy
- Farce, slapstick-lite, pratfall (no real injury)
- Workplace, bureaucratic, academic, scientific
- Parody or spoof of a genre the setup names
- Nonsense or non sequitur only if framed as play, not as confusion

## Do not default to

- Roast, put-down, mockery, ridicule of a named private person
- Stereotype or ethnic humor as the engine
- Shock, gross-out, or transgressive humor unless the user named that register and the target stays fictional
- Tickling, peekaboo, or infant play as adult story engines
