# Humor craft notes

Adapted from the user's attached *The Science of Humor and the Architecture of the Funny* (65-page research synthesis, August 2026), especially chapters 2, 4, 5, 8–11, and 13. These are craft heuristics, not a formula guaranteeing laughter.

- Establish a familiar expectation before breaking it. Use incongruity, reversal, category error, literal reading, role reversal, or exaggerated commitment. Supply a clear inferential bridge for a satisfying reinterpretation; absurdist stories may deliberately leave a playful mismatch unresolved.
- Keep violations safely playable through fictional distance, low stakes, self-directed embarrassment, or an obviously implausible premise. Surprise alone can yield confusion or distress.
- Cross-classify devices freely: deadpan is delivery, satire a critical mode, a pun a linguistic mechanism, and a callback a structure. Pick devices for the brief rather than cramming all of them into one story.
- Build beats through setup, an initial comic turn, varied heightening, and a callback that changes the meaning of an earlier detail. The rule of three can establish a pattern twice and break it the third time. Avoid repeated beats that add no new information.
- Use specificity and timing: concrete details create expectation; brief pauses, short paragraphs, and sentence-final reveals shape when readers revise that expectation. Avoid lengthy explanation after a punch line.
- Consider target, audience, power, and shared knowledge. Favor affiliative humor and self-aware characters. A joke at another person's expense may change meaning when power or consent changes.
- Diagnose weak passages: no surprise; surprise without a playful frame; obscure context; excessive explanation; too much processing; repetitive escalation; or a target whose distress becomes the only source of amusement.
