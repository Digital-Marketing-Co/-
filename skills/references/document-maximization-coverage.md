# Document Maximization and Omission Prevention — Execution Standard

## Purpose
Maximize substantive completeness, explanatory depth, scientific rigor and usability for the requested scope; never maximize word count or page count alone. This is the canonical non-duplication standard, augmenting document-error-prevention.md and post-artifact-itqe-latex-qa.md.

## Automatic /disabled /p policy
When /disabled /p or "until exhaustively complete" is invoked, proceed through all safe authorized stages without routine confirmation. Use iPhone-first outputs, low interaction, checkpoints, preservation of originals, and zero paid inference where requested. Do not ask repetitive questions. Stop on missing authorization, unavailable evidence or essential destructive/production approval; report blockers and partial completion. No promise of indefinite background execution.

## Preflight — omission threat model
1. Read complete available source, previous versions, attachment pages, references, requested scope and latest constraints. Record unavailable sources explicitly rather than invent content.
2. Build a coverage manifest: requirement ID, source location, claim/equation/table/figure/citation identifiers, expected section, evidence provenance, confidence, completion and QA status.
3. Inspect for omissions in dimensions relevant to subject: definitions, causal mechanisms, alternatives, confounders, edge cases, boundary conditions, temporal/geographic/market variation, accessibility, privacy/security, externalities, cost and feasibility, uncertainties, counterarguments, safety, implementation, validation and references.
4. Treat the claim "without error of omission" as a testable aspiration, not proof of unknowable universal completeness. Preserve relevance; avoid spurious invented factors.

## Generation and expansion
5. Map each requirement to one or more identifiable paragraphs/tables/figures; construct a traceability matrix that detects missing coverage.
6. Reconcile all original content against the source manifest before and after each /expound iteration. No accidental summarization, silent deletion, unexplained shrinkage, duplicate content, stale PDF, broken image or citation.
7. Use /4629 for selected visuals, /ITQE and /latex for mathematics, /ivy-/biblio single citation registry, /copyright single footer controller, and /karma for verified recurring defects. Link to shared standards rather than copying their implementation.
8. Expand only where new value is evidenced: derivations, assumptions, comparisons, worked examples, sensitivity, counterexamples, uncertainty and falsifiability. Mark theoretical or invented illustrative data explicitly.

## Quantified completeness and release
9. Report total requirements, mapped requirements, verified requirements, unresolved requirements, original and output section counts, words/tables/figures/equations/citations and source-to-output deltas. A 100% coverage score means only declared manifest requirements are mapped, not that all possible knowledge is covered.
10. Inspect full final output, including endings, headings, footnotes, tables, appendices, links, metadata, math and pages. Run format-specific visual and structural checks; prevent clipping, truncation, conflicting style layers, duplicated footers and inaccessible text.
11. Run two consecutive complete evidence-backed QA passes with zero major/critical outstanding defects; when blocked mark NOT TESTED or BLOCKED. Do not label a theoretical audit as an executed test. Preserve original backups and hashes.
12. Add a /karma defect event, minimal regression fixture, root cause and preventive correction for actual reproducible omissions. Do not call deterministic rule updates trained machine learning.
13. End once scope is satisfied or bounded by explicit blockers; avoid unbounded recursion, endless paraphrase cycles, irrelevant content inflation or false exhaustive claims.

## Test fixtures
Use fixture A: input with three sections, one equation and one appendix, verify all survive export. Fixture B: duplicate footer and bibliography entry, detect and deduplicate. Fixture C: late revision of conclusion, verify regenerated PDF uses latest source. Fixture D: deliberately missing limitation/confounder, detect via coverage manifest. Fixture E: inaccessible source, report blocker instead of making up coverage. Fixture F: long DOCX/PDF with tables and images, validate every page and ending. Only mark fixtures passed after actual execution with evidence.

## Human-readable output
Present the real final artifact plus concise QA manifest: requirements matched, omitted/unavailable areas, rendered pages inspected, repairs made, two-pass state, and saved paths. No internal skill-command names in reader-facing document body unless the topic explicitly concerns the system itself.
