# Research protocol

Saturate the published record that search and page tools can reach. Saturation is operational, not omniscience.

## Source tiers

1. Primary documents — statutes, treaties, hearings, archival digital objects, official statistics, patents, technical manuals. Prefer the issuing body’s PDF.
2. Ivy League and peer university-press monographs and journal articles (Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Columbia, Penn, Brown, Dartmouth, Cornell, plus Chicago, Oxford, Cambridge, MIT, Stanford when they own the field).
3. U.S. government and military (`.gov`, `.mil`, GAO, CRS, NARA, LOC, NIH, NSF).
4. Peer-reviewed journals from other research universities and learned societies.
5. Reputable reference works.
6. High-quality journalism only when it reports a document you cannot otherwise reach.

Do not treat SEO blogs, content-farm listicles, or unsourced social posts as authorities.

## Rounds

Each round writes `rounds/round-N.md`.

- Round 1 — map the field.
- Round 2 — primary and Ivy core (`site:harvard.edu` and the other seven, plus `.gov`).
- Round 3 — rival schools, critiques, later revisions.
- Round 4+ — named holes only. Stop a hole after two empty targeted searches.

Record keepers in `sources.jsonl` with tier, kind, title, creator, year, publisher_or_venue, url, accessed, claim_ids.

Never invent a page number. If a book is cited everywhere but the full text is closed, cite from a catalog or preview and say so in the note.
