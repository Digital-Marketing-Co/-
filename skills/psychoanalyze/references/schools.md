# School battery

Run every cluster against the artifact. If a cluster has no purchase, say so in one sentence and move on. Do not pad.

Primary titles below are search targets. Cite only volumes actually opened in the run. Do not invent page numbers.

## 1. Freud — drive, dream-work, defense

Targets — *Interpretation of Dreams*; *Psychopathology of Everyday Life*; *Three Essays*; *Beyond the Pleasure Principle*; *The Ego and the Id*; *Inhibitions, Symptoms and Anxiety*; case studies as method, not as templates to force onto new names.

Look for condensation, displacement, secondary revision, negation, slips, jokes, repetition, and the pairing of wish and prohibition in the diction. Dream-work applies only when the artifact is a dream-report or uses explicit dream rhetoric.

## 2. Ego psychology and Anna Freud

Targets — Anna Freud, *The Ego and the Mechanisms of Defence*; Hartmann on adaptation.

Inventory defenses that the diction itself performs — repression-as-omission, denial, projection, isolation of affect, intellectualization, undoing, turning against the self, reaction formation, sublimation. Name the defense only when a quoted span shows the operation.

## 3. Klein and early object relations

Targets — Klein, *Envy and Gratitude*; notes on the paranoid-schizoid and depressive positions; projective identification.

Look for splitting (all-good / all-bad nouns), envy of an imagined full object, reparation language, and sudden collapses from idealization to attack.

## 4. Winnicott

Targets — *Playing and Reality*; true and false self papers; holding and handling.

Look for transitional objects and spaces, compliance as a false-self style, play versus compliance, and fear of breakdown announced as form rather than content.

## 5. Bion

Targets — *Learning from Experience*; *Experiences in Groups*; container / contained; alpha function; beta elements; basic assumptions (dependency, fight-flight, pairing).

Use Bion on groups when the artifact is an organization, a comment thread, a manifesto, or a meeting log. Beta elements are undigested images that the text repeats without thinking.

## 6. Lacan

Targets — *Écrits*; Seminars I, II, VII, XI; Imaginary, Symbolic, Real; desire versus demand; *objet petit a*; Name-of-the-Father; the subject supposed to know.

Do not translate Lacan back into ego psychology. Look for the gap between what the text demands and what it can name, for master-signifiers that hold the piece together, and for points where the Symbolic fails and the text loops.

## 7. Jung

Targets — *Collected Works* on archetypes, shadow, anima / animus, Self, individuation; *Memories, Dreams, Reflections* only as a source on method.

Look for mythic personae, shadow projection onto a collective enemy, and compensatory images. Do not invent an archetype that the images do not support.

## 8. Adler, Rank, Ferenczi

Short pass. Inferiority and social interest (Adler). Birth and will (Rank). Mutuality and trauma of the analyst’s presence (Ferenczi). Use only if the artifact argues those themes.

## 9. Kohut — self psychology

Targets — *The Analysis of the Self*; mirroring, idealizing, and twinship transferences as textual demands.

Look for texts that need an echoing you, an idealized banner, or a twin. Grandiosity and shame oscillations belong here more than in Kleinian envy when the wound is self-cohesion.

## 10. Attachment and mentalization

Targets — Bowlby; Ainsworth patterns as *heuristics not diagnoses*; Fonagy and Target on mentalization; Main on narrative coherence.

Look for narrative coherence, sudden breaks in reflecting on other minds, and protest / dismiss / tangle styles in how the text handles other people. Never assign an Ainsworth category to a living author.

## 11. Relational and intersubjective

Targets — Mitchell; Benjamin (*Bonds of Love*, recognition and destruction); Aron.

Look for doer / done-to pairs and failed mutual recognition.

## 12. Existential and phenomenological

Targets — May; Yalom as popularizer only with a caveat; Boss; Binswanger.

Look for death, freedom, isolation, and meaning as named stakes rather than as inferred drives.

## 13. Critical, feminist, queer

Targets — Fromm; Marcuse; Chodorow; Irigaray; Kristeva (*Revolution in Poetic Language*, abjection); Butler only where the artifact stages sexed citation; Mitchell’s *Psychoanalysis and Feminism*.

Look for how the text genders authority, abjects a body, or repeats a kinship plot.

## 14. Colonial and racialized scenes

Targets — Fanon, *Black Skin, White Masks* and *Wretched of the Earth*; Bhabha on mimicry and hybridity.

Required when the artifact stages race, colony, nation, or a civilizational “we.” Do not treat Fanon as a footnote to Freud.

## 15. Group, institution, organization

Targets — Bion basic assumptions; Menzies Lyth on social defense; Turquet; Kernberg on organizations only as published theory.

Use on companies, agencies, churches, parties, comment sections, and house brands.

## 16. Literary and aesthetic method

Targets — Felman; Brooks (*Reading for the Plot*); Bloom’s anxiety of influence as a theory of texts, not of patients; Kris on the aesthetic.

When the artifact is art, start here and let clinical vocabularies arrive second.

## 17. Neuropsychoanalysis (short)

Targets — Solms; Panksepp affective systems as *models*, not scans of the author.

One section at most. Do not fake a brain image.

## How to write a school paragraph

1. Quote or describe the span.
2. Name the school’s term.
3. Say what the term explains that H0 does not.
4. Say what would count against the reading.
5. Cite a real keeper.

If step 3 is empty, the paragraph is padding. Delete it.
