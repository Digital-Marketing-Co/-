# Ethics, power, and target (monograph Ch. 4–6)

Whether a joke is the same joke cannot be decided by words alone. Speaker, target, audience, hierarchy, and deniability change the social act.

## Default target ladder (use in this order)

1. The narrator or protagonist (self-deprecating, self-enhancing)
2. An object, tool, form, policy, or genre convention
3. A fictional type or institution
4. A public-facing convention the reader already shares (airport security, group chats, printers)
5. Never a named living private person as the fool
6. Never a minor as a comic sexual or violent target

## Humor styles (Martin et al.)

- Affiliative — default. Bonds the reader to the protagonist.
- Self-enhancing — allowed. Coping without attacking.
- Aggressive — only at systems, conventions, or consensual roast frames the user asked for.
- Self-defeating — use sparingly. Cost to the self should stay play-framed, not depressive.

## Benign-violation check

A violation and a benign appraisal must be simultaneously available. If only the violation remains, the draft is not a /hilarious story.

## Occupational and high-stress subjects

Military, medical, emergency, and legal settings can use gallows color only as coping among peers, never as mockery of the injured, the dead, or a subordinate. Leadership-style downward sarcasm is banned.

## Consent inside the scene

Teasing among equals can create intimacy. Identical wording from a boss to a worker is discipline. Write the former unless the user asked for satire of the latter, in which case the laugh is at the boss-system, not at the worker.
