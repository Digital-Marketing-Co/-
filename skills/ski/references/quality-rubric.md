# Source and claim quality rubric
Assess each source separately for design fit, sampling/selection, measurement validity, blinding/interrater reliability, confounding, missing data, analysis/multiplicity, transparency and generalizability. Use low/some/high concern or unclear, with reasons. Do not award prestige points. Reviews inherit limitations of included work; authoritative consensus is not experimental proof.
At claim level use: robust (convergent sound replicated evidence); provisional (limited/heterogeneous studies or correlation); consensus (identified professional guideline); hypothesis (plausible untested mechanism); unsupported (popular assertion not established by retrieved evidence). Record directness, consistency and uncertainty alongside the label. A single small study never becomes robust solely through statistical significance. Describe effect estimates and uncertainty when verified; do not invent missing effect sizes.
Keep signal function distinct from receiver response, latent emotion from observable movement, learning from language, clinical referral populations from population prevalence. Assess human emotion labels critically: eliciting a situation does not validate the dog's subjective state. Compare protocol details before treating conflicting experiments as failures to replicate. Separate welfare, efficacy and efficiency outcomes.
