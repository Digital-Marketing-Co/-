# Training curriculum contract

## Shared plan fields
For every competency record ID, essential/optional/specialist category, purpose, prerequisites, observable baseline and success criteria, environment, equipment, preferred reinforcers, sequential teaching steps, progression/regression criteria, generalization, maintenance, troubleshooting, welfare safeguards and evidence/consensus IDs. Explicitly identify which thresholds and sequences are practical design choices. Record session-level response latency, opportunities/successes, stress observations, trigger distance/intensity, recovery, prompt level and change decision. Provide writable individual dog, competency and session templates.

## Inventory
Include distinct plans for name response, attention, recall, emergency recall, settling, relaxation/rest, stationing, house training, comfortable confinement, loose leash, doors/gates, leave it, drop it, waiting, disengagement, grooming, handling, veterinary care, muzzle conditioning, human greetings, dog interactions, gradual independence, separation distress management, safe play, developmentally appropriate bite inhibition, enrichment and species-typical outlets. Add optional trick/scent/sport activities and specialist service/detection tasks only with prerequisites, qualified supervision and limits. Distinguish adolescent, puppy, adult, senior and disability adaptations.

## Welfare and learning
Teach with reinforcement defined by its demonstrated effect on future behavior, not by the trainer's intent. Use shaping/capturing/lures when appropriate; fade prompts deliberately and avoid coercive positioning. Change one difficulty dimension at a time, vary contexts gradually and lower criteria after failure or discomfort. Give rest, water and opt-out opportunities. Do not withhold essential nutrition or access to bodily needs. Do not require eye contact or a sit from dogs for whom these are painful or difficult.
Address fear through subthreshold exposure and positive prediction, never forced prolonged exposure. A refusal of food is relevant but neither necessary nor sufficient for stress diagnosis. Protect escape routes and warning signals. Do not provoke aggression to test it. Arrange safety and seek veterinary/qualified behavior support for bite risk, pain, severe fear or separation distress. Teach independent skills only below observed distress threshold; do not leave a panicking dog to cry it out. Medical needs and emergency care may require a veterinary sedation/handling plan rather than a training rehearsal.
